## 1. Project Overview

This repository accompanies the research study entitled *The Secret in Symbols: The Intellectual Dimensions of Chen Shen's Laozi Pinjie and the Evolution of Ming Dynasty Commentary*.

The study investigates Chen Shen's *Laozi Pinjie* (1590), a representative work of Ming dynasty commentary. It examines the historical, literary, aesthetic, and intellectual dimensions of Chen Shen's commentarial approach, emphasizing the importance of commentary symbols and classification methods in understanding classical Chinese philosophical texts.

The research reconsiders *Laozi Pinjie* as both a philosophical commentary and an independent form of literary criticism.

## 2. Research Objectives

- Examine the evolution of Laozi commentary during the Ming dynasty.
- Investigate Chen Shen's biography and the historical editions of his work.
- Analyze the commentary symbols and classification methods used in *Laozi Pinjie*.
- Explore the three-category, seven-grade classification system.
- Examine the influence of traditional Chinese calligraphic aesthetics.
- Investigate the relationship between literature (Wen) and the Way (Dao).
- Evaluate the contribution of Chen Shen's commentary to Chinese literary criticism.

## 3. Main Research Contributions

1. Historical reassessment of Ming dynasty Laozi commentary.
2. Examination of Chen Shen's distinctive commentarial practices.
3. Analysis of the three principal categories: Jiapin (Excellent), Miaopin (Marvelous), and Shenpin (Divine).
4. Investigation of the seven-grade aesthetic classification system.
5. Exploration of the relationship between textual interpretation, literary aesthetics, and philosophical reflection.
6. Reassessment of the intellectual significance of *Laozi Pinjie* within late Ming literary culture.

## 5. Historical and Intellectual Context

The study situates Chen Shen's commentary within the broader development of Ming dynasty intellectual and literary culture. Particular attention is given to the flourishing of commentary practices during the late Ming period and the growing emphasis on individual expression, literary quality, and naturalness.
